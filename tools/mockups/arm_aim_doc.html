Arm aim: the write-up

Owner, 2026-10-29: "Shot is ok to deviate from muzzle but we want muzzle to aim as close as possible to shooting point." The OpenSpec change is openspec/changes/arm-aim. Nothing here is in the game yet: this page is the design.

What the pilot sees

You look at something and the mech's guns swing onto it. The arms move first, quicker than the torso, so on a sudden turn you see the barrels come round past the screens ahead of the cabin. Then they settle back as the torso catches up. On a close target the two guns angle in toward each other. Rounds always land under the crosshair, as now; what changes is that they now leave along the barrel instead of at an angle to it.

How it works today

  • The arms have no aim of their own. They hang at their pose on the torso, and the torso turns toward your look at 70 deg/s (twist) and 40 deg/s (pitch).
  • A shot flies from the muzzle to the point under the crosshair. It hits what you aim at, but it leaves the barrel at whatever angle the gun happens to make: wide while the torso is turning, and wide on any close target.

The solve

  • Each gun arm aims with three joints: the shoulder's pitch and yaw, and the elbow's pitch. They are posed on shoulder_* and forearm_*, never upper_arm_*, which carries no mesh.
  • The barrel is the muzzle socket's forward axis. Each frame, four steps of damped least squares turn it onto the line from the muzzle to the target. Each joint stays in its limits, with a light pull back to rest, so a target in reach is met with the least bend.
  • The arm moves toward that answer no faster than its slew rate. Whatever the joints cannot reach is left as error, shown in the readout above.
  • A striking arm lets go of its aim and takes it back after the strike. Recoil plays over the aimed pose.
  • An arm whose muzzle does not point forward at rest is a launcher and is not aimed. The Sapper's left pod fires upward; its missiles steer themselves.

The 0.1 to 0.3 deg left over is the idle sway, which the arms follow at their slew.

Cost: per gun arm, per frame, a handful of small matrix products and one 3 by 3 solve. That is estimated at well under a microsecond in Rust, and nothing on the GPU. It is not measured: there is no game code yet, and the cloud session has no GPU.
