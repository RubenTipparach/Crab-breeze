Destructible city block

Mockup for the OpenSpec changes building-generation, building-interiors, structural-destruction and rubble. One seed generates a 1980s block from rules: mass, storeys, structural grid, facade, roof clutter, floorplans and furniture. Every piece belongs to one element of a structural graph, so shooting a column moves load onto its neighbours, an overloaded column creaks and fails, unsupported floors fall, and what lands turns into rubble piles you could walk on. Orbit with the mouse and click a building to fire. The pilot (1.8 m) and a 12 m mech stand in the street for scale.

What is the real design here

  • One seeded stream (SplitMix64 forked by label, the same as cb-core::rng) builds everything: the same seed gives the same block.
  • One structural grid (6 m) is the column layout, the facade's bay rhythm, the line partitions follow, the spatial index for blasts and the graph destruction runs on.
  • Each building draws in one call per material. A destroyed piece costs one bit in a per-building mask the vertex shader reads, so no mesh is rebuilt when something breaks.
  • Collapse is a load path: floors put weight on the columns at their corners, columns pass it down, and a column carrying more than its capacity (2 x its intact load) fails after a short creak.
  • Rubble is a heightfield that grows where pieces land and slumps to a 37 degree angle of repose, with loose pieces on top.

What is a mockup stand-in

  • Textures are canvas noise. The game uses Material Maker graphs for brick, concrete and roofing, and face-on Cycles renders of modelled facade bays (Star Crew's method) for trims and far facades.
  • Debris pieces do not hit standing walls or each other. In the game Avian does that (debris-physics).
  • Furniture and roof props are boxes at their real sizes, not the Blender kit.
  • Frame times shown are this browser's, not a measurement (CLAUDE.md section 7).
