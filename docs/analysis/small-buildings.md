# Houses and small businesses: research and sources

Research behind `openspec/changes/small-buildings`, 2026-10-24. The setting is an alternate-history
1980s (survey G1), so the buildings are what stood in a working town around 1985: post-war houses,
roadside commerce from the 1930s to the 1970s, and regional building traditions where the island's
biomes call for them.

## What makes each type read at a glance

| Type | The tells, from the cockpit | From the sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ranch house | long and low, hip roof, picture window, attached garage | the most built American house form 1945-1975 (McAlester) |
| Colonial (Revival) | symmetric five bays, centre door, shutters, end chimneys | McAlester, Colonial Revival |
| Craftsman bungalow | gable to the street, deep porch on tapered posts over brick piers | McAlester; Lancaster, *The American Bungalow* |
| Split-level | living wing beside a raised bedroom wing over the garage | McAlester, "Split-Level" |
| A-frame | roof to the ground, the gable all glass | Randl, *A-Frame* |
| Pueblo Revival | battered stucco, rounded parapets, vigas, portal porch | McAlester; Bunting, *Early Architecture in New Mexico* |
| Stilt house | raised on piles over flood, veranda, steep metal roof | the raised houses of the tropics and the Gulf, e.g. the Queenslander and Cajun raised cottage |
| Beach cottage | pastel siding on piers, louvres, screened porch | Gulf and Florida coast vernacular |
| Single-wide trailer | long ribbed box, two-tone stripe, skirting, steps | Wallis, *Wheel Estate* |
| Streamline diner | stainless, enamel bands, rounded ends, ribbon windows, counter and booths | Gutman, *American Diner Then and Now* |
| Gas station | canopy over islands, kiosk, price pylon, brand colours | Jakle and Sculle, *The Gas Station in America* |
| Corner store | brick, shop front with transom, awning, flat over it, cornice | Liebs, *Main Street to Miracle Mile* |
| Auto repair | block, roll-up doors, sign band | Liebs |
| Motor court motel | rooms off an open walkway, door, window and AC per room, pool, neon pylon | Jakle, Sculle and Rogers, *The Motel in America* |
| Strip mall | one long block, a shingled mansard canopy (very 1970s-80s), four shops | Liebs; Hess, *Googie Redux* |
| Burger drive-in | swept roof rising to the street, glass front, arrow sign | Hess, *Googie Redux* |
| Quonset hut | corrugated half-cylinder, block end wall | Decker and Chiei, *Quonset Hut* |

## How they are built so they look right

- **Openings are holes, not decals.** The wall is cut around each opening, and the frame, sill and
  glass sit in the reveal. The wall's thickness shows at the jambs, which is what makes a window
  read as real at 20 m. The interior shows through the glass.
- **Materials carry the type**: brick coursing at true scale (75 mm courses, 225 mm bricks), 200
  mm clapboard exposure, 400 x 200 mm block, 76 mm corrugation. Textures tile in world metres,
  so a brick is a brick on every building.
- **Paint and wear vary by building, not by material.** One material per surface kind, with a
  per-building tint, keeps a whole town to about 45 draws.
- **Life sells it**: cars from the period, an air conditioner in a window, a TV antenna, a chimney,
  neon at night.
